Build a village. Prepare your heroes. Venture into the unknown. Know what you're risking, but never know for certain how the story will end.

## Why I'm Making This Game

Wanderlust: Triumph is a personal project built around a simple ambition: to create the fantasy game I would most love to play myself.

I want a world that makes me curious about what lies beyond the next hill. A world of vivid landscapes, forgotten places, strange discoveries, and rugged beauty. But I also want the pleasure of mastering a deep game system: developing heroes, experimenting with builds and equipment, assembling parties, and discovering combinations that change how you approach a challenge.

I'm interested in the meeting point between these experiences: a world worth exploring, a village worth protecting, and a system worth understanding.

I'm developing the game independently, in my spare time. I'm not trying to rush it toward a deadline. I want to keep refining the mechanics, atmosphere, and connections between them until they deliver the experience I'm aiming for.

## The Risk Should Be Visible Before You Commit

One of the ideas at the heart of Wanderlust: Triumph is that preparation should give you useful information, not certainty.

Before an expedition, you can inspect its danger band, reward tier, expected duration, and an estimate of how your actual party is likely to fare. That forecast uses Monte Carlo simulation to evaluate the group you've assembled, rather than giving you a generic prediction detached from your choices.

It doesn't promise that your heroes will succeed. The estimate is not the outcome. Unexpected events and uncertainty still matter.

But it gives you something valuable before you commit: a way to compare opportunities, understand the risks, and decide whether the potential reward is worth the danger.

Your party should influence what happens without making every expedition a foregone conclusion. The aim is to make risk understandable, preparation meaningful, and outcomes worth reflecting on.

## Build, Prepare, Venture Out

Wanderlust: Triumph connects village management with dangerous expeditions.

You manage limited resources, assign people to activities, develop your heroes, and decide which opportunities deserve your attention. When an expedition appears, you must consider the party, its strengths and weaknesses, the available equipment, the danger, and what you stand to gain.

I'm building toward substantial depth in character development, builds, equipment, itemization, and the interactions between them. I want different choices to create different approaches, not simply different numbers.

The expedition itself is about making decisions under uncertainty, dealing with consequences, and deciding when the potential reward justifies the risk. The focus is on strategic preparation and meaningful outcomes, rather than presenting the game as a conventional animated tactical-combat RPG.

And defeat is meant to be part of the larger journey. The long-term direction includes a run-based progression in which the loss of a settlement or the end of an attempt can contribute to what comes next. Failure should be painful, but it should also leave something behind that helps shape a future attempt.

## The Games That Shaped My Thinking

Several games have influenced different parts of this vision:

- **Lords of Waterdeep** inspired my interest in assigning limited resources and balancing competing priorities.
- **Cultist Simulator** showed how experimentation and interactions between systems can make discovery rewarding.
- **They Are Billions** inspired the pressure that comes from preparing under constraints while threats loom.
- **Darkest Dungeon** captures the difficult choice between pushing onward and retreating before the cost becomes too high.
- **Magic: The Gathering** is a lasting inspiration for synergies, combinations, and the pleasure of discovering what a well-built system can do.

These are inspirations, not blueprints. I want to take the ideas that excite me and bring them together into a different experience: a fantasy world where strategic preparation, character development, risk, and discovery reinforce one another.

## A World Built Around Wonder

The visual direction matters just as much as the systems.

I'm drawn to fantasy art with vivid color, dramatic light, expressive painterly textures, expansive landscapes, and a sense of wild beauty. I want the unknown to inspire curiosity as well as apprehension. The world should feel like somewhere you want to explore, not just a succession of dangers to overcome.

I've researched painting styles, individual artists, visual references, and color palettes to develop a direction of my own. I've also used AI image generation as a tool for exploring and refining possibilities against that research.

The goal isn't to generate generic fantasy art. It's to pursue a specific artistic vision through deliberate research, selection, and iteration.

The moodboard communicates the atmosphere I'm working toward. Its references are not necessarily finished game assets, nor a promise that every location will look exactly like them. They're a way to share the visual identity I want to develop.

## A World That Remembers

My longer-term ambition is for adventures to leave a mark. I want past decisions, losses, discoveries, and surviving characters to contribute to the story that unfolds next.

I also want the end of a run to be part of a larger progression, rather than a simple wall that erases the value of everything that came before. The intended direction is for what survives or is earned to help shape future attempts.

These are development goals, not claims that every part of this vision is already implemented. The work now is to build the systems that can support them and make the experience coherent.

## Follow the Development

I'm sharing this project because I'd like to find people who care about the same things: deep RPG systems, unusual builds, meaningful preparation, dangerous expeditions, and fantasy worlds that inspire wonder.

I'd love to hear what you think about the direction, the mechanics, and the art. Which kinds of places would make you want to venture farther? What makes a risk feel fair? What makes a build or item combination satisfying to discover?

Specific criticism is welcome. I'm making the game I want to play, but that doesn't mean I have nothing left to learn.

Wanderlust: Triumph is a work in progress, built one step at a time. If this is the kind of fantasy game you'd like to explore, I'd be glad to have you along.
